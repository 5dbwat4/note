But really, scaling laws are these simple, predictive rules of how to go from small scale model performance and behavior, and to try to extrapolate them up to large scale behavior. And so the basic naive way to approach model training might be to say, I have a big budget, I'm going to do multiple training runs on these very big runs, and I'm going to tune hyperparameters on those big runs, but that's very wasteful. So instead, what I would like to do is I would like to do all of my optimization at the small scale and have some simple rule that allows me to extrapolate small scale behaviors to large scale behaviors. And if the small scale and large scale are connected by some very simple, robust connections like regularities of some kind, then you would have confidence in this kind of new scaling approach to optimizing your system. And that's really what today is all going to be about-- this engineering view of a scaling law.

但实际上，缩放定律是一些简单、具有预测性的规则，告诉你如何从模型在小规模下的性能和行为外推（extrapolate）到大规模行为。一种朴素的方法可能会说：我有很大的预算，我要在这些非常大的训练运行上做多次运行，并在这些大运行上调整超参数——但那非常浪费。所以取而代之，我想做的是：在小规模下完成所有的优化，并找到一个简单的规则让我能够将小规模行为外推到大规模行为。如果小规模和大规模之间存在一些非常简单、稳健的联系——比如某种规律性——那么你就会对这种新的规模化优化方法有信心。这就是今天全部要讲的内容——缩放定律的工程视角。

批大小，从之前的课中知道，还存在另一个原因使其非常棘手。我们希望批大小尽可能大，因为大的批大小给我们并行化的机会——记住，数据并行（data parallel）需要大的批大小。讲了这么多，我们真正想知道的是：在开始受到损害之前，我们能把批大小做到多大？这是相关的系统问题。你还想知道这如何随模型大小变化。

So given that preface, there is this very important idea that is used in a variety of papers and talked about very frequently, called the critical batch size. The critical batch size is roughly this idea that up until a certain point, when you start to get diminishing returns, there is returns, perfect returns, to increased batch size. So I might call this the noise-limited regime. So in the noise-limited regime, every additional element that you throw in your batch reduces the gradient noise in your SGD step. And since you're variance-limited, that reduction in variance is very, very helpful. That gives you big returns. So this is the regime in which you have perfect scaling. You're doing about as well as you can, given the amount of extra examples that you're processing. As you get to a certain point where you've reached the noise scale of your gradients, you get to a point where you are no longer variance-limited. You're now bias-limited. What does that mean? Well, remember, in gradient descent, you're looking at the local structure of your objective. You don't have a global view of where the minimum is. So no matter how low noise I am, I'm only going to point in this light blue direction over here. And there's always a disagreement between my local descent direction and where the global optimum is. Because of that, at a certain point, I'm going to get diminishing returns because now my limiting factor is no longer variance. It is this bias term. So now I'm bias-limited past this point over here. And so critical batch is kind of a rule of thumb, you might call it, or a convenient trade-off point where you say this is the point at which we're starting to cross over from our perfect scaling regime to our ineffective scaling regime. And so it gives you a good rule of thumb of where to be setting your batch size if you want your batch size to be as big as possible without suffering these huge efficiency losses. Hopefully, that idea is very clear.

在这个前提下，有一个非常重要的概念，在各种论文中使用并被频繁讨论：**临界批大小（critical batch size）**。临界批大小大致是这样的想法：在到达某一特定点之前，增加批大小有完全的回报——之后开始出现递减回报。我可以称之为**噪声受限区域（noise-limited regime）**。在噪声受限区域中，你在批次中每增加一个额外元素都会减少 SGD 步骤中的梯度噪声（gradient noise）。由于你是方差受限的（variance-limited），方差的减少非常非常有帮助，给你带来很大的回报。所以这是你拥有完美缩放（perfect scaling）的区域——你在给定处理的额外样本数量下，做尽可能好。当你到达某个点，达到梯度的噪声尺度（noise scale）时，你不再方差受限——你变为**偏差受限（bias-limited）**。这意味着什么？记住，在梯度下降中，你在看目标的局部结构，你没有一个关于最小值在哪里的全局视角。所以无论我的噪声多低，我只能指向这个浅蓝色方向。我的局部下降方向与全局最优之间存在分歧。因此，在某一点之后，我会得到递减的回报——因为现在我的限制因素不再是方差，而是这个偏差项。所以过了这个点，我就偏差受限了。临界批大小你可以称之为经验法则或者一个方便的权衡点——这个点是我们开始从完美缩放区域过渡到无效缩放区域的地方。因此，如果你希望批大小尽可能大又不想承受这些巨大的效率损失，它为你提供了一个设定批大小的好经验法则。希望这个概念非常清楚。

Now, critical batch size is kind of a strange object. It is derived from a variety of complicated calculations about the local quadratic approximations to the objective. But I will maybe give you the mechanical steps of what is actually used to estimate this quantity. So what you do is first, you pick a target loss. Target loss might be some numbers that you want to hit. So your goal is to hit this target loss as quickly as possible. That's my goal right now. What I'm going to do is I'm going to sweep over all the different batch sizes or number of different batch sizes, and I'm going to write down the number of steps that I needed to reach my target loss, and the number of examples that were needed to get to that target loss. Of course, number one and two are related by a batch size factor. The number of steps times my batch size is equal to the number of examples. Now, through some calculations, OpenAI folks, McCandless and others, argue that this is the rough functional form that we should follow, that the number of steps that you need and the number of examples that you need are inversely related, with this estimate, which is the minimum number of possible steps and the minimum number of examples, normalizing these two quantities. And so there's a trade-off between these two. If you want to minimize the number of examples, you're going to have a lot of steps. And if you want to minimize the number of steps, you're going to need a lot of examples. That's a very natural trade-off. And if you increase or decrease your batch size, you're going to have trade-offs that go one side or the other.

现在，临界批大小是一个有点奇怪的对象。它来自于关于目标局部二次逼近（local quadratic approximations）的各种复杂计算。但我可以给你实际用于估计这个量的机械步骤。首先，你选择一个目标损失（target loss）——你想达到的某个数值。你的目标是尽可能快地达到这个目标损失。你要做的是扫描所有不同的批大小或一定数量的不同批大小，记下达到目标损失所需的步数以及达到该目标损失所需的样本数量。当然，第一和第二通过批大小因子相关：步数乘以批大小等于样本数。通过一些计算，OpenAI 的 McCandless 等人论证了这是我们应遵循的大致函数形式——所需的步数和所需的样本数成反比关系，通过这个估计量（最小可能步数和最小可能样本数）对这两个量进行归一化。所以这两者之间存在一个权衡：如果你想最小化样本数，你会需要很多步；如果你想最小化步数，你会需要很多样本。这是一个非常自然的权衡。如果你增加或减少批大小，你会得到偏向一侧或另一侧的权衡。

Now, they argue the goal now should be to balance these two sides. I want to make sure that my number of steps term and the number of examples terms are roughly balanced with each other. And if you solve for that, you're going to get this particular solution, which is that the critical batch size is the minimum number of examples that I need divided by the minimum number of steps that I need. And these two are estimated by fitting this to the observed data that I have. This is going to balance both sides of the equation. It's going to give me a little bit more number of steps than optimal. It's going to require a little bit more examples than optimal, but it balances the two sides. And you can think of this as optimizing the convergence rate without blowing up the number of steps that you need. There's another added point that I won't really belabor because this gets even more into the weeds. You should really read the critical batch size paper if you're interested into this, where one of their arguments is estimating this is quite complicated. You can do this by just looking at the ratio of the gradient covariance in the squared norm of your gradient.

他们论证现在的目标应该是平衡这两方面。我确信我的步数项和样本数项大致相互平衡。如果你求解这个，你会得到这个特定的解：临界批大小等于所需的最小样本数除以所需的最小步数。这两个量是通过对我观察到的数据进行拟合来估计的。这将平衡方程的两边——它会比最优需要多一点步数，也会比最优需要多一点样本数，但它平衡了两边。你可以认为这优化了收敛速度而不会让所需步数爆炸。还有一个额外的点我不再赘述，因为这会更深入细节。如果你对此感兴趣，应该阅读临界批大小的论文，其中一个论点是估计这个量相当复杂。你可以通过观察梯度协方差（gradient covariance）与梯度平方范数（squared norm of gradient）的比率来做到这一点。

But if you're scaling up a recipe that you don't want to scale up, your warmup is kind of crazy, or your batch sizes are crazy, you're going to get bad scaling laws. So you want to be as close to the proper full run as possible.

所以我认为每一个看起来都是非常微小的差异，但如果你改变这些非常微小的差异，你可以在缩放定律中得到巨大的变化。我想强调的一点是：缩放定律在某种意义上是下界（lower bound）——它们有点像在说：如果我继续这个配方并扩大规模，那么这就是我将得到的。但如果你在扩大一个你实际上不想扩大的配方——你的预热有点疯狂，或者你的批大小很疯狂——你会得到不好的缩放定律。所以你要尽可能接近适当的完整运行。

---

There's another paper that does another very interesting thing to try to study the difference between Kaplan and Chinchilla scaling laws. It's by Pearce and Song. And what they do, and I think this is very clever, is they don't train any models. They just take the Chinchilla training curves or the implied functional form of the Chinchilla training curves. They just plot them out and they say, well, based on these implied training curves, which are simulated purely from Chinchilla, they generate what the implied training curves will be for Kaplan if you ignore the embedding parameters and you scale way down, and what happens if you use the total parameter count instead. And what they find is really two effects. One is that Kaplan is operating at a much lower compute scale, so they're very sensitive to small changes, and actually ignoring the non-embedding parameter is a sufficiently big change to mess up the result relative to Chinchilla. So their argument is slightly different from Yair and others, which is that here, actually, it's the size of the compute scale plus the slight non-linearity induced by non embedding parameters that's causing this disagreement. So this is really starting to get into the weeds. But I hope you appreciate the importance of paying attention to detail. And I think a lot of the work that goes on in establishing, relying, scaling laws is about figuring out what the right regime is so that you can get reliable scaling across compute scales.

还有另一篇论文做了另一件非常有趣的事情来研究 Kaplan 和 Chinchilla 缩放定律的区别——由 Pearce 和 Song 完成。我认为他们的做法非常聪明：他们不训练任何模型。他们只是取 Chinchilla 的训练曲线或隐含函数形式，把它们画出来，然后说，基于这些完全从 Chinchilla 模拟生成的隐含训练曲线，他们生成如果忽略嵌入参数并大幅缩小时 Kaplan 的隐含训练曲线会是什么样子，以及如果使用总参数量会怎样。他们发现的主要是两个效应。一是 Kaplan 在低得多的计算规模下操作，因此他们对微小变化非常敏感，而忽略非嵌入参数是一个足够大的变化，会搞乱相对于 Chinchilla 的结果。所以他们的论点与 Yair 等人略有不同——实际上是计算规模的大小加上非嵌入参数引起的轻微非线性导致了这种分歧。这真的开始深入细节了。但我希望你们能理解关注细节的重要性。我认为在建立和依赖缩放定律方面的大量工作都是关于找出正确的区域，以便你能够获得跨计算规模的可靠缩放。

---

The final thing, and this is really just more of something that I just find amusing more than anything else, which is that Chinchilla is this paper that takes down the Kaplan paper in a way. It's like, oh, you guys were very wrong. But there was this one remaining mystery, which is that Chinchilla method three never agreed with Chinchilla method 1 and 2. And the numbers look small. And the authors are very unperturbed about this, but I think the implications are quite non-trivial. If you look at this, methods 1 and 2 basically say the amount of parameters and tokens should scale together. So there should be a fixed ratio between the two. This is where the Chinchilla factor of 20 comes from. If you look at number 3, the exponents are different. This means if you scale up your compute, eventually, actually, in this case, you're going to have way more tokens than you are going to have parameters. So it's going to be actually a very different scaling conclusion asymptotically than the other ones. And so it turns out that I was certainly not the only one who was perturbed about this, except some folks at Epoch AI did something about being perturbed. They couldn't get either the raw data or the code. So what they did was they extracted the data from plots in the paper, and they refitted the method 3 regressions. They extracted the data from the paper. And given that data, they fitted that 2D surface or 3D surface that was proposed. And then, what they showed is actually the original paper must have underfitted the original data. And if they redid the curve fitting, they could get much lower losses relative to what the proposed scaling law in the Chinchilla paper. And in fact, if you follow the refitted model, you got almost exactly D over 20 is 20 rule of thumb from the Chinchilla paper, which is really a funny conclusion, I think, to the Chinchilla paper, where in the end it turns out that the authors were more right than they knew. Actually, they had made a mistake, and that was the only reason why method 3 disagreed from methods 1 and 2. So it's a nice conclusion to know that the method was robust after all.

最后一件事，对我来说这只是觉得有趣多于其他：Chinchilla 这篇论文在某种程度上推翻了 Kaplan 论文——"哦，你们错得很厉害。"但是有一个悬而未决的谜团：Chinchilla 的方法 3 从未与方法 1 和方法 2 一致。数字看起来很小，作者对此并不在意，但我认为影响非平凡。方法 1 和 2 基本上说参数量和标记数量应该一起缩放——所以两者之间应该有一个固定比例，这就是 Chinchilla 的 20 倍因子从何而来。而方法 3 的指数不同，这意味着如果你扩大计算量，最终你会拥有比参数量多得多的标记数——所以渐近线上它会是一个非常不同的缩放结论。结果发现，我绝对不是唯一对此感到不安的人——Epoch AI 的一些人为此做了些事情。他们无法获取原始数据或代码，所以他们从论文的图中提取数据，重新拟合了方法 3 的回归。基于这些数据，他们拟合了提出的那个 2D 或 3D 曲面，然后展示了原始论文一定是对原始数据欠拟合了。如果他们重新做曲线拟合，他们可以得到比 Chinchilla 论文中提出的缩放定律损失低得多的结果。事实上，如果你遵循重新拟合的模型，你几乎正好得到 Chinchilla 论文中 D 除以 20 为 20 的经验法则。我认为这对于 Chinchilla 论文来说是一个有趣的结论——最终发现作者比他们自己知道的更正确。实际上，他们犯了一个错误，而这正是方法 3 与方法 1 和 2 不一致的唯一原因。所以知道这个方法最终还是稳健的，是一个不错的结论。

OK, so that's the end of the Chinchilla saga in some ways. But I will end this section by saying you probably don't want the Chinchilla factor. At this point, I think this is a pretty common knowledge thing. But if you're training a production model, you don't really care about saving training compute for the most part. You can look at all sorts of external surveys that people have done of how Frontier Labs are spending their compute. Most of the compute is not going into training runs. Most of the compute is actually going into R&D and serving. And given that, especially for serving, what you want to do is you want small models that are capable. You do not want big, bloated models that cost a lot to serve, even if that minimizes training cost. And so what you really want is something that you would call as "overtrained." I put that in quotes because, really, overtraining is what we want. That's the right amount of training. And if you look at all the models that were released in the early days, like GPT-3-- this was under-trained for sure, three tokens per parameter. Chinchilla gets to the modern ratio of 20 per parameter. And then for a while, people were at 20 per parameter. And this is the era, I think, where there wasn't that much serving of these models. The models were cool, but it wasn't being served at scale. And then as you start to move to eras where serving becomes very real, now you're overtraining these models, and now you've moved into MoEs, and you're making all these trade-offs to optimize inference serving. So I think Chinchilla is important, not because I think the 20-to-1 ratio is the one golden ratio. If you're doing stuff for research, it might be. But I think the reason why it's important is because it tells us quite a bit about how we should be fitting scaling laws and how to think about scaling laws.

好了，从某种意义上说，Chinchilla 的故事就到这里了。但我要以这句话结束这一部分：**你可能不需要 Chinchilla 因子**。到了这个阶段，我认为这是一个相当常识性的事情。但如果你在训练一个生产模型，你大多数情况下并不关心节省训练计算。你可以看各种外部调查，了解前沿实验室是如何花费他们的计算资源的——大部分计算并没有用于训练运行，而是用于研发（R&D）和推理服务（serving）。鉴于此，特别是对于推理服务，你想要的是**小而有能力的模型**，而不是又大又臃肿、服务成本高昂的模型，即使这样最小化了训练成本。所以你真正想要的是被称为"过训练（overtrained）"的东西——我加了引号，因为实际上过训练就是我们想要的，那才是正确的训练量。如果你看早期发布的所有模型，比如 GPT-3——它肯定是训练不足的——3 个标记每参数。Chinchilla 达到了现代比例 20 每参数。然后有一段时间，人们都在 20 每参数。我认为这个时代这些模型的服务量还不大——模型很酷，但没有大规模服务。然后当你进入服务变得非常真实的时代，现在你过训练这些模型，进入 MoE，并做所有这些权衡以优化推理服务。所以我认为 Chinchilla 重要，不是因为我认为 20 比 1 是唯一的黄金比例——如果你在做研究，它可能是。我认为它重要的原因是它告诉了我们很多关于我们应该如何拟合缩放定律以及如何思考缩放定律的信息。
